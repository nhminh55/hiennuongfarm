  steps:
    - title: Agricultural by-products
      name: |-
        Agricultural
        by-products
      lead: Carrying on the value of rice straw.
      text:
        - After each rice harvest, local rice straw is put to use as material for growing black termite mushrooms. Instead of being thrown away, the by-products become the input for a new production cycle at Hiền Nương.
        - The materials are checked before use, ready for treating and mixing into the substrate. From materials familiar to every rice field, the farm adds value through growing mushrooms.
    - title: Preparing the substrate
      name: |-
        Preparing
        the substrate
      lead: A nourishing base for the mushrooms to grow.
      text:
        - The straw is finely chopped and mixed with rice bran and corn bran to make the mushroom-growing substrate. The mix is packed into bags and steamed before the spawn is added.
        - Fineness, moisture and the quality of the materials are kept in check during preparation. The treated substrate is what feeds the mycelium in the next step.
    - title: Spawning & incubation
      name: |-
        Spawning
        & incubation
      lead: Nurturing the mycelium from within.
      text:
        - Mushroom spawn is added to the treated substrate, and the bags are set aside to incubate while the mycelium grows. As it spreads through the substrate, each bag becomes a spawn bag ready for growing.
        - Hiền Nương produces its own spawn and spawn bags on site, following their growth and checking their quality before they go into the grow houses or to growers who need them. Producing its own spawn lets the farm control what goes in and support growers with technical guidance.
    - title: Growing & harvesting
      name: |-
        Growing
        & harvesting
      lead: Tending the bags, welcoming each flush.
      text:
        - Once the mycelium has grown, the black termite mushroom bags are opened and covered with a layer of cleaned, sterilised sand. The grow houses are kept at suitable humidity and temperature, with a mist of clean water to help the mushrooms grow.
        - The mushrooms are watched and harvested over several flushes in each growing cycle. When the cycle ends, the remaining substrate moves on to the next use.
    - title: Substrate for red worms
      name: |-
        Substrate for
        red worms
      lead: After the mushroom season, the substrate stays useful.
      text:
        - After a growing cycle, the substrate is gathered, freed of its plastic bags and put to use as feed for red worms. Material that once nourished mushrooms becomes the input for raising worms.
        - As the worms digest the organic material, the farm gains worm castings for its crops. This step draws more value from the spent substrate and reduces what has to be thrown away.
    - title: Worm castings for crops
      name: |-
        Worm castings
        for crops
      lead: Returning organic nourishment to the soil.
      text:
        - Red worm castings are used as organic nourishment for crops; at Hiền Nương they are also mixed with cow manure for fertilising. The product of this step is an organic fertiliser, kept separate from raising the worms.
        - The nourishment goes on to feed the soil and the crops. When the crops leave suitable by-products, those materials can be used in the next production cycles — carrying the journey on from by-products to mushrooms, from substrate to fertiliser, and back to the soil.
